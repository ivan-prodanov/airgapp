Privacy

What this site knows about you.

Not much, on purpose. No cookies, no analytics, no trackers, no ads. The only thing this site ever asks you for is an email address, and only if you request the iPhone beta.

Last updated 10 November 2026

Who's responsible

Airgapp and this site are made by Ivan Prodanov. For anything on this page, including the rights at the bottom, write to privacy@airgapp.net.

When you visit

This site and the demo are hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web server, GitHub sees your IP address and browser details when your browser asks for a page, and keeps them in its logs to run and protect the service. I don't get those logs. GitHub explains what it keeps in its privacy statement.

Everything else a page needs, from fonts to videos, comes from this site itself. Nothing loads from Google or an ad network, and nothing is saved in your browser.

The demo

The console demo at demo.airgapp.net runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you tap, type or switch on there is sent anywhere, and it all resets when you reload.

The iPhone beta

If you ask for the iPhone beta, you give me your Apple ID email. I use it for one thing: inviting you to the TestFlight beta. I rely on your consent, and you can withdraw it at any time by writing to privacy@airgapp.net.

Here's where your email goes:

  • Web3Forms delivers the form to my inbox. It handles your email on my behalf under its data-processing agreement, from India, with the EU's standard contractual safeguards for data leaving the EU. To catch spam it sends your email and IP address to CleanTalk and Akismet, and it keeps a copy of the request for a limited time (at most three years). Its privacy policy has the details.
  • Me. I add your email as a tester in Apple's App Store Connect, which sends you the invite. Then I delete your request from my inbox.
  • Apple keeps your email for as long as you're in the beta, under its privacy policy. You can leave the beta in the TestFlight app whenever you like, or ask me to remove you.

The Android download

The Android app downloads from GitHub, which logs the download like any other request. Nothing else about you is involved.

The app itself

Airgapp, the app, has its own privacy policy.

Your rights

You can ask to see the data I hold about you, to correct it or to delete it, and you can withdraw your consent or object to how it's used. Write to privacy@airgapp.net and I'll answer within a month. If you're not happy with the answer, you can complain to the data-protection authority where you live.
